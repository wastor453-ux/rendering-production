# VOICEOVER SCRIPT — "Your Savings Are Dying While You Sleep"
### Tilbury-format adaptation · faceless · ~10.5 minutes · ~1,550 words
### Rules: no filler openers · [pause: 300ms] / [pause: 500ms] between clauses · every number phonetic · clean numerals live on cards only

---

## HOOK (0:00–0:35)

One dollar in nineteen eighty-five buys what two dollars and eighty cents buys today. [pause: 500ms] Your savings lost two thirds of their value while you did absolutely nothing. [pause: 300ms] That is inflation. It never takes a day off. [pause: 500ms] Now the twist. [pause: 300ms] One man invested two hundred dollars a month for ten years, then stopped forever. He retired with eight hundred thirteen thousand dollars. [pause: 500ms] His friend invested eighty-four thousand dollars more than him, and finished poorer. [pause: 300ms] The difference was never money. It was time.

[CARD: $1.00 → $2.80 decay line, loss red]

---

## BEAT 1 — THE SILENT THIEF (0:35–1:40)

Right now, doing nothing with your money is one of the riskiest things you can do. [pause: 300ms] I know how that sounds. [pause: 300ms] Leaving it in the bank feels safe and responsible, because the number on the screen never changes. [pause: 500ms] But that number is lying to you.

Think about a cup of coffee. [pause: 300ms] Or a chocolate bar. [pause: 300ms] Think about what they cost ten years ago, and what they cost today. [pause: 500ms] They did not get better. If anything, they got smaller. [pause: 300ms] What actually happened is your money got weaker.

Here is the math nobody shows you. [pause: 300ms] A single dollar in nineteen eighty-five had the same buying power as two dollars and eighty cents today. [pause: 500ms] Stuff a dollar under your mattress in the eighties, and it lost almost two thirds of its value without you lifting a finger. [pause: 300ms] No crash. No scam. No mistake. [pause: 500ms] Just time, quietly taxing you.

Money that sits still is slowly dying. [pause: 500ms] And investing is the only way to put it to work while you sleep.

[CARD: "MONEY THAT SITS STILL IS SLOWLY DYING" — purchasing-power decay curve]

---

## BEAT 2 — THE LADDER (1:40–3:40)

Let me show you what "putting it to work" actually means, with one million dollars. [pause: 500ms]

Leave that million in a normal bank account earning next to nothing. [pause: 300ms] After a year, you have made pretty much nothing. [pause: 500ms]

Move it into a high-yield savings account at around three percent. [pause: 300ms] After a year, you have made thirty thousand dollars. [pause: 300ms] Respectable. [pause: 500ms]

Now invest that same million in the stock market at around ten percent a year. [pause: 300ms] Not guaranteed, but historically possible. [pause: 300ms] After a year, you are looking at one hundred thousand dollars. [pause: 500ms]

Nothing. [pause: 300ms] Thirty thousand. [pause: 300ms] One hundred thousand. [pause: 500ms] Same money. Three completely different fates.

[CARD: three cards — BANK ≈ $0 · HYSA 3% = $30,000/yr · MARKET 10% = $100,000/yr]

Now, you are probably thinking you do not have a million dollars. [pause: 300ms] Fair. [pause: 300ms] But here is the secret the wealthy understand and beginners miss: [pause: 500ms] it was never about the starting amount. It is about something called compound interest. [pause: 300ms] And to show you how violent it is, meet Billy and Jack.

[CARD: "IT WAS NEVER ABOUT THE AMOUNT" — transition]

---

## BEAT 3 — THE TIME ARBITRAGE (3:40–6:10)

Billy starts investing two hundred dollars a month at twenty years old. [pause: 300ms] He earns ten percent a year. [pause: 300ms] And at thirty, he stops. [pause: 500ms] Never adds another penny for the rest of his life.

Jack waits. [pause: 300ms] He starts at thirty. [pause: 300ms] And to make up for lost time, he invests eighty-four thousand dollars more than Billy ever did. [pause: 500ms]

Who wins? [pause: 500ms]

Billy, who quit at thirty and invested eighty-four thousand dollars less, retires with eight hundred thirteen thousand dollars. [pause: 500ms] Jack retires with six hundred seventy-eight thousand. [pause: 500ms]

Read that again. [pause: 300ms] The man who stopped investing for decades beat the man who invested more money for longer. [pause: 500ms]

That is the whole game, in one story. [pause: 300ms] It is not about how much you put in. [pause: 300ms] It is about how long you give it to grow. [pause: 500ms]

Now the honest warning. [pause: 300ms] For the first ten, fifteen, maybe twenty years, this feels painfully slow. [pause: 300ms] You will check your account and think, is this even doing anything? [pause: 500ms] That feeling is normal. [pause: 300ms] And it is the exact moment most people give up and cash out — right before the magic kicks in. [pause: 500ms]

Think of it like a snowball. [pause: 300ms] At the start you are shoving it uphill by hand, and it barely moves. [pause: 300ms] Then one day it tips over the top, and it starts rolling downhill on its own, gathering more snow — more money — than you could ever push alone. [pause: 500ms]

So money grows when it owns things instead of sitting in cash. [pause: 300ms] But what exactly should it own? [pause: 500ms] That is where the industry lies to you.

[CARD: dual bars — BILLY $813,000 vs JACK $678,000 · caption: "time beats money"]

## BEAT 4 — THE LIE IN THE LABEL (6:10–8:10)

Everyone will tell you to buy an S and P five hundred index fund. [pause: 300ms] It is safe, they say. [pause: 300ms] Sensible. [pause: 300ms] Diversified across five hundred companies. [pause: 500ms] And most of that is true.

But here is what they do not tell you. [pause: 500ms]

When you buy that fund, you assume your money spreads evenly across five hundred companies. [pause: 300ms] It does not. [pause: 500ms] Most S and P funds are weighted by market capitalization, which means the bigger the company, the bigger its slice of your money. [pause: 300ms] Apple. [pause: 300ms] Microsoft. [pause: 300ms] Nvidia. [pause: 300ms] A handful of mega-cap tech giants quietly swallow a massive share of the entire index. [pause: 500ms]

So your "diversified" five-hundred-company fund is, in reality, a concentrated bet on the biggest companies in the world. [pause: 500ms] If they keep growing, wonderful — they are the reason the index has performed so well. [pause: 300ms] But if the giants fall, they drag the entire index down far harder than five hundred equal companies ever would. [pause: 500ms]

There is an equally-weighted version that fixes this. [pause: 300ms] But because it is not riding the giants, do not expect it to perform anywhere near as well. [pause: 500ms]

To be clear: owning hundreds of companies is still dramatically safer than betting on one or two stocks. [pause: 300ms] Just know what you actually own. [pause: 500ms] The label says diversification. [pause: 300ms] The fine print says concentration.

[CARD: pie chart — top 7 holdings vs the other 493 · caption: "THE LABEL SAYS 500. THE FINE PRINT SAYS 7."]

## BEAT 5 — THE FIVE MISTAKES (8:10–10:00)

After forty years in these markets, the same five mistakes destroy beginners over and over. [pause: 300ms] Memorize them, and they will never happen to you. [pause: 500ms]

Mistake one: panic selling at the bottom. [pause: 300ms] When the market drops, fear takes over and people sell everything to stop the bleeding. [pause: 300ms] But a drop is not a loss until you sell. [pause: 500ms] People panic-sell on Monday and watch the stock bounce back on Wednesday, turning a temporary dip into a permanent disaster. [pause: 500ms]

Mistake two: the exact opposite — FOMO buying at the top. [pause: 300ms] It is all over the news, everyone seems to be getting rich, so you pile in at the peak. [pause: 300ms] Days later, it crashes. [pause: 500ms] Never chase hype. And on the rare occasion you touch it, only with fun money you are prepared to lose. [pause: 500ms]

Mistake three: diamond-handing a dying company. [pause: 300ms] There is a difference between holding a good business through a rough patch and clinging to a broken one because you refuse to admit you were wrong. [pause: 500ms] Patience is a strategy. [pause: 300ms] Stubbornness is an expense. [pause: 500ms]

Mistake four: chasing the next Nvidia. [pause: 300ms] Everyone wants to find the next ten-bagger. [pause: 300ms] But hunting for it makes your brain see patterns that are not there, and that hallucination is expensive. [pause: 500ms] Slow and steady has beaten clever for forty years. [pause: 500ms]

Mistake five: accepting high fees. [pause: 300ms] This one is invisible, which makes it the most dangerous. [pause: 300ms] One or two percent a year sounds like nothing. [pause: 500ms] But compounded over thirty or forty years, that tiny fee swallows a fortune — a fortune that was avoidable with a commission-free platform. [pause: 500ms]

Five mistakes. [pause: 300ms] Zero of them are about picking the wrong stock. [pause: 300ms] Every one of them is about behavior.

[CARD: five swipe cards — PANIC SELL · FOMO BUY · DIAMOND-HAND THE DYING · CHASE HYPE · PAY 2% FEES]

## BEAT 6 — THE CHALLENGE (10:00–10:30)

You now know more than most people ever learn about money. [pause: 300ms] But knowledge without action is just entertainment. [pause: 500ms] So here is your thirty-day challenge.

This week: open the account. [pause: 300ms] Next week: automate a small monthly investment — even fifty dollars counts. [pause: 300ms] Then leave it alone, and let time do the violent work. [pause: 500ms]

Billy started at twenty with two hundred a month. [pause: 300ms] You start today, with whatever you have. [pause: 500ms] The clock is the asset. Start it.

[CARD: "THE CLOCK IS THE ASSET. START IT." — 30-day checklist card]
