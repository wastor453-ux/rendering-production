# Demo video script — "How $500 a Month Becomes $1 Million"
Format: spoken-form text for TTS. One file per scene.

## Scene 1 — HOOK
Five hundred dollars a month. Thirty years. One million dollars. Here is the math nobody shows you.

## Scene 2 — THE SETUP
Invest five hundred dollars every single month into a low cost index fund. Not a lump sum. Not timing the market. Just five hundred dollars, on autopilot, month after month.

## Scene 3 — THE CURVE
At an eight percent average annual return, your total contributions are one hundred eighty thousand dollars. But compounding turns that into seven hundred forty-five thousand. Watch the curve. The first ten years feel painfully slow. The last ten years do most of the work. Compound interest is just math, but it behaves like magic.

## Scene 4 — TIME VS TIMING
Now look at what ten years costs you. Start at twenty-five, and forty years of compounding builds one point seven million dollars. Start at thirty-five, invest the exact same five hundred a month, and you get seven hundred forty-five thousand. Ten years of waiting cost one million dollars. Time beats timing, every single time.

## Scene 5 — THE FEE TRAP
One more thing. A one percent annual fee sounds tiny. But over forty years, it drags your one point seven million down to one point three million. That tiny fee quietly cost you over four hundred thousand dollars.

## Scene 6 — CLOSE
The best time to start was ten years ago. The second best time is today.

## Scene 2b — WHERE 8 PERCENT COMES FROM
Where does that eight percent come from? The United States stock market has averaged about ten percent a year for over a century. After inflation, that is roughly seven to eight. We use eight to stay conservative. Past performance never guarantees future results, but history is the best map we have.

## Scene 5b — DON'T PANIC SELL
And all of this assumes one thing. That you never panic sell. The average investor earns far less than the market itself, because they buy high and sell low. Automation fixes this. Set it, forget it, and let compounding do the heavy lifting.
